The idea

Use connectivity to guide the placement.

When a direct transfer has no shared grasp, an intermediate pose can connect two transfers. The incoming and outgoing grasps can differ.

Compose adjacent-pose scores

The energy model evaluates grasp feasibility at a pose. Composing the two endpoint energies gives a differentiable pair score Q. Summing these scores guides the intermediate poses.

Jseq = Q(Tinit, Tmid) + Q(Tmid, Tgoal)

For longer sequences, the sum extends over every adjacent pair. Verification-driven search starts with a short sequence and increases its length after analytical failure.

Propose poses

Optimize position and yaw within fixed stable placement modes, with matched initialized candidates.

Verify connections

Check actual shared grasps, inverse kinematics and collisions. Use full motion planning for executable robot paths.

Optimize the motion

Combine connectivity guidance with an explicit motion objective, retaining the paths that pass physical verification.

Generation and ranking have different roles.

The balanced objective adds a penalty for unequal adjacent-edge scores. It changes where candidates are generated. The ordinary score can then rank the saved candidates. The benchmark supports this combination in pooled validity, with object-level exceptions including Bottle and Mug.

Setup and how to read these examples

These illustrations use the 10k-pose models, the canonical FPS20 grasp pools, 200 candidates and 20 updates. The first registered one-step query for each object is used. The quantitative one-step benchmark uses 1000 candidates and ten updates on its registered query set.

What changes during optimization?

The intermediate pose and internal joint paths can move continuously. Motion checks can also accept a replan, change the shared-grasp pair, or select another candidate.
